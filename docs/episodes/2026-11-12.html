First, from The Verge: Google DeepMind Unveils AlphaGenome 2 for Disease Research. Google DeepMind has unveiled AlphaGenome 2, a new AI model designed to reveal how changes in DNA affect different cells. As the successor to AlphaGenome, it gives scientists a more detailed view of the cellular roots of disease, a notoriously difficult problem that could hold clues to disease causes and point the way to new treatments. Why it matters: Major foundation model release from Google DeepMind with direct implications for genomics, drug discovery, and biomedical research.

Second, from Google: Google Launches Gemini Decisions API for Developers. Google introduced the Gemini Decisions API, powered by Gemini Decision, a state-of-the-art model built for fast, calibrated multimodal decisions. The API gives developers a way to integrate AI-driven decision-making into applications across multiple modalities. It represents Google's latest push into practical AI developer tools, targeting use cases where reliable, multimodal judgment is needed to automate or support choices in software. Why it matters: New Google AI product giving developers multimodal decision-making capabilities for applications and enterprise automation.

Third, from The Verge: Nvidia Board Reportedly Preparing to Oust CEO Jensen Huang. Nvidia's board is reportedly preparing to dismiss founder and CEO Jensen Huang following reports of alleged extramarital affairs, including one with a woman alleged to be a Chinese intelligence agent and another with an Nvidia executive. The Wall Street Journal reported the board could act soon, and Nvidia's stock dropped on the news. Huang has led the company for 33 years and is central to its AI chip dominance. Why it matters: Leadership change at Nvidia, the central AI infrastructure company, would affect AI chips, data centers, and the entire AI supply chain.

Fourth, from Ars Technica: Google DeepMind Uses AI to Verify Signal Encryption. Google DeepMind mathematically verified key parts of Signal's encryption in a four-week project, suggesting AI could make formal verification of cryptographic protocols far more practical. The work applies AI to security-critical software verification, an area traditionally requiring extensive manual effort. If scalable, the approach could improve assurance in encryption and other security systems. Why it matters: Demonstrates AI's potential to make formal verification of security-critical software practical at scale.

Why it matters: Major funding deal connecting the world's leading contract chipmaker with two top AI labs amid market volatility.
